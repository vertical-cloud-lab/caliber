| Counting statistics (Poisson; two independent spectra) | For a 1 wt% difference at 95% confidence, the counting-only requirement is about **426 net Si Kα counts per spectrum** if background is negligible: \(1.96\sqrt{5^2/N+6^2/N}\le1\). A conservative target of **≥1,000 net counts** gives an approximate 95% uncertainty of **±0.32–0.37 wt% per spectrum** at 5–6 wt% Si; **10,000 counts** gives about **±0.10–0.12 wt%**. Background subtraction increases the requirement. Modern high-count SDD spectra readily exceed this level (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000004) | Random; reducible by counts and replicates | **No**, once ≥1,000–10,000 net Si counts are collected; geometry, microstructure, and particle sampling then dominate |
| Standards-based k-ratio/matrix correction on a flat, polished specimen | Si repeatability of **0.079% relative** for single measurements on K411 glass; approximately **0.21% relative** across 20 locations. Fe–3Si gave **0.24% relative** replicate precision. Across 50 major/minor determinations, **84% were within ±2% relative** of reference values—about **±0.10–0.12 wt% at 5–6 wt% Si**—although routine accuracy guidance is broader (about ±10% relative for minor constituents) (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | Small random repeatability plus systematic standards/matrix-model uncertainty | **Usually no** for a controlled polished mount, but a measured 1 wt% difference should be confirmed by batch QC and preferably bulk chemistry |
| Loose-particle geometry at **10 kV** | For approximately 0.9–18 µm glass spheres, particle/bulk ratios were generally within **0.9–1.1** above about 1.4 µm; irregular-shard composition variation was about **8–12% relative**, corresponding roughly to **0.4–0.7 wt% Si** at 5–6 wt% (pqac-00000075, pqac-00000076, pqac-00000077) | Particle-position-dependent random scatter with systematic size/shape bias | **Potentially**, especially without size matching, center targeting, filtering, or particle-specific correction |
| Loose-particle geometry at **15 kV** | Mass-effect loss reached about **13%** for a 1.8 µm sphere; particle/bulk response approached unity only above roughly 3 µm. Low-energy Si Kα absorption artifacts were larger than at 10 kV, so biases of order **≥0.6 wt% at 5 wt% Si** are plausible for small particles (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000076) | Systematic with particle diameter and detector-facing geometry; additional random placement scatter | **Yes** for small or broadly sized loose particles |
| Loose-particle geometry at **20 kV** | Direct 20 kV values were not isolated in the retrieved microsphere results; behavior lies between 15 and 25 kV. At 20 kV, a 2–3 µm sphere is penetrated through much of its volume, with electron escape and edge generation; at 25 kV, 1–2 µm spheres lost **50–75%** of high-energy intensity and Si particle/bulk ratios reached about **1.4**. Thus 20 kV can readily cause **tens-of-percent relative** errors for particles only a few micrometres across (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000072) | Strong systematic size/shape/absorption bias plus position-dependent scatter | **Yes—often overwhelmingly** for loose particles comparable to the interaction volume |
| Peak-to-background (P/B) correction on loose powder | Earlier particle P/B work commonly achieved about **10–20% relative error**; a modern modified P/B workflow on 74 raw powder materials generally achieved **5–10% relative deviation**, equivalent to roughly **0.25–0.60 wt% at 5–6 wt% Si**. It reduces but does not eliminate tilt, absorption, substrate, roughness, and finite-size errors (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000044) | Residual systematic bias plus particle-to-particle scatter | **Borderline**: adequate for screening a 1 wt% shift with many accepted particles, but weaker than polished-section analysis |
| Within-particle Al–Si microstructural heterogeneity | AlSi10Mg powder had mean secondary dendrite-arm spacing **2.19 ± 0.56 µm**; Si is enriched in the interdendritic/eutectic network, while rapidly solidified α-Al can be supersaturated and contain nanoscale Si. Point analyses can therefore range from Al-rich cells to Si-rich eutectic by many wt%; spatial morphology and spacing also change within one droplet (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000059, pqac-00000060, pqac-00000065) | Real spatial heterogeneity; random only if locations are sampled without bias | **Yes** for spot analyses; largely controlled by a representative raster spanning many cells or whole cross-section |
| Particle-to-particle compositional scatter | No universal value exists for atomized Al–Si powder. Modern automated powder EDS found 48.1% of benchmark elemental distributions with SD <1 at% and 91.2% <2 at%, but these data cover diverse chemistries, not specifically Al–Si. Particle size, nucleation, solute trapping, and cooling history can create real between-particle differences (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000064, pqac-00000071) | Real random-effects variance, potentially size-dependent | **Often yes**; particle count must be selected from the observed SD, not from counting statistics alone |
| Sampling and size segregation in jars/containers | Sampling-method variability reported as **6.81% for quartering**, **5.14% for scoop sampling**, **1.01% for chute splitting**, and **0.146% for rotary division**. Vibration and flow can segregate particle sizes; fine Al–Si droplets cool faster and exhibit more solute trapping/less segregation, so a size gradient can become an apparent top–bottom chemistry gradient (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000040, pqac-00000065) | Systematic lot-location bias plus sampling variance | **Yes** unless each jar level is independently split with a rotary divider and results are stratified or matched by particle size |
