| Technique/acquisition choice | Quantitative fidelity effect | Interpretation for metal-alloy microanalysis | Source context IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEM-EDS: standards-based vs standardless** | Physical standards can support about **±5% relative uncertainty**; standardless analysis can reach **±30%**, and normalization to 100% can conceal errors. | Standards are needed for qualification-grade alloy chemistry; standardless results should be treated as semi-quantitative. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020) |
| **SEM-EDS: accelerating voltage/overvoltage** | ISO 22309 specifies incident energy of at least **1.8× the selected X-ray-line energy**; adequate Fe-K excitation, for example, requires at least **12 kV**. Measurements at **5, 10, and 20 kV** produced substantial composition scatter in a low-energy-line test material. | Voltage determines excitation efficiency and interaction volume; one fixed voltage is not valid for every alloying element. | (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) |
| **SEM-EDS: take-off geometry** | Quantitative protocols require a flat surface normal to the beam and approximately **35° to the EDS detector**. Low take-off angles at holes and grooves reduced integral counts and the number of detected elements. | Tilt, roughness, pores, and melt-pool relief can create element-dependent absorption errors, especially for low-energy Mg and Al lines. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000019) |
| **SEM-EDS: beam current/dead time** | Beam current and dead time were identified as dominant variables; **10–20% dead time** was recommended. In standards-based work, beam-current drift was held to **0.2%** over a **20 min** session. | Too little current gives poor counting statistics, while excessive rate causes pile-up and dead-time losses; current must be measured or controlled. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000020) |
| **SEM-EDS: normalized standardless NIST Fe-Cr-Ni alloy** | At **20 kV**, errors were **+1.3% Cr, −0.4% Fe, and −0.8% Ni**; on another system at **25 kV**, they were **−3.1%, +1.3%, and −4.1%**. | Even favorable major-element results vary with detector calibration, fitting, matrix corrections, and factory databases. | (pqac-00000019) |
| **SEM-EDS: unnormalized standardless vs standards** | Unnormalized standardless analysis gave **−0.7% Cr, −2.3% Fe, and −2.7% Ni** and totaled **98.1 wt%**. Physical standards reduced errors to **<1%** against NIST EDS values, although residual uncertainty versus wet chemistry was **3–7%** because of ZAF corrections. | Standards cancel much of the beam-current, geometry, detector-efficiency, and contamination dependence, but matrix-correction uncertainty remains. | (pqac-00000020) |
| **SEM-EDS: Mg in LPBF AlSi10Mg** | **Evidence gap:** no controlled SEM-EDS parameter sweep for minor Mg in LPBF AlSi10Mg was found. General evidence places standardless uncertainty as high as **±30%**, with greater risk for low-energy/light-element peaks. | Apparent Mg differences in AlSi10Mg cannot reliably be assigned to metallurgy without alloy- and instrument-specific validation. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000020) |
| **EBSD: scan speed—transferable metal evidence** | Increasing speed from **54 to 154 patterns/s** reduced conventional indexing success from **34.9% to 9.3%**. At 54 patterns/s, processing raised success to **41.3–59.7%**; at 154 patterns/s, improved values reached approximately **12.5–37.6%**. | Faster mapping can turn most pixels into non-indexed or unreliable measurements and make grain statistics cleanup-dependent. | (pqac-00000009) |
| **EBSD: frame averaging/exposure—transferable metal evidence** | Tests used **1, 5, 10, 20, and 30 frames**. One-frame patterns were almost entirely misclassified; improvement began at five, reasonable classification appeared at ten, and nearly all patterns were correct at 20–30. Five instead of 30 frames yielded a **6× speed gain** with high accuracy for most phases. | Exposure must be optimized against throughput rather than minimized; map appearance alone does not establish fidelity. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) |
| **EBSD: accelerating voltage—transferable metal evidence** | At **10, 20, and 30 kV**, 10 kV caused many misclassifications, 20 kV was the default, and 30 kV gave reasonably good performance. | Lower voltage can improve spatial resolution but reduce indexing reliability unless current, exposure, and indexing are re-optimized. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) |
| **EBSD: resolution/binning—transferable Al evidence** | Al 7075 was mapped at **5, 10, and 20 kV** using **4×4 and 8×8 binning**. Another study tested **156×128, 622×512, and 1244×1024** patterns; low resolution retained high accuracy for most phases but affected similar structures more strongly. | Binning accelerates acquisition but removes diffraction detail; suitability is phase- and microstructure-dependent. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) |
| **EBSD: phase-list selection—transferable duplex-alloy evidence** | With two correct phases, Hough indexing produced a high-fidelity map. Expanding the list to **12 candidates** caused **zero patterns** to index as either correct phase despite ideal acquisition settings. | Phase-list choice can overwhelm improvements in beam conditions and generate confident but false phase maps. | (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) |
| **EBSD: LPBF Al-Si-specific evidence** | **Evidence gap:** retrieved LPBF AlSi10Mg studies did not provide controlled factorial data for indexing rate or confidence versus current, voltage, exposure, binning, and phase list. | Published settings are examples, not validated optima; systematic LPBF Al-Si acquisition-response data are needed. | (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016) |
| **Micro-XRF: voltage/current/filters** | A commercial alloy protocol used **15–50 kV**, **12–106 µA**, and automatic filters of **75 µm Cu/25 µm Ti/200 µm Al**, **38 µm Al**, **25 µm Ti/300 µm Al**, or **25 µm Fe**. A **60 s** measurement comprised separate **30 s** light- and heavy-element scans. | Excitation must be element- and matrix-specific; a high-voltage transition-metal setting is not automatically sensitive to Mg, Al, or Si. | (pqac-00000028) |
| **Micro-XRF: current, optics, and filter sensitivity** | Tests used **40 kV/0.2 mA**, increased current from **0.2 to 1.0 mA** on Ag-Cu alloy, and evaluated **12.5–50 µm Mo** and **50–200 µm Al** filters. Polycapillary spectra were **35–40×** more intense than monocapillary spectra, with flux-density gain up to approximately **300×**. | Current controls photon statistics until rate limits intervene; filters and optics reshape both background and elemental sensitivity. | (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) |
| **Micro-XRF: detection limits** | At **40 kV/0.2 mA** and **1000 s**, minimum detection limits were typically **10–100 ppm**; examples included **23–68 ppm Mn**, **10–22 ppm Rb**, and **68–94 ppm Ca**, depending on tube and optics. | Trace sensitivity cannot be transferred between instruments without documenting tube, optics, filter, atmosphere, detector, and live time. | (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) |
| **Micro-XRF: matrix correction/calibration** | With suitable matrix-matched calibration, typical errors can fall below **5% for major** and **6% for some minor elements**. Without suitable standards, errors were typically below **50% for major/minor** and up to **100% for trace elements**, with some exceeding 100%. | Fundamental-parameter corrections do not remove matrix dependence; alloy-specific reference materials remain necessary. | (pqac-00000007) |
| **XRF: Mg errors in Al alloys—transferable to AlSi10Mg risk** | In AlMg4.5Mn0.7, EDXRF over-reported Mg by about **2 wt%**, approximately **40% relative**. In nominal Al99.5, it reported **0.196 wt% instead of 0.004 wt%**, a **5000% relative deviation**. | Minor/trace Mg can be grossly biased in commercial alloy modes. These cases are XRF—not SEM-EDS—and are not LPBF-specific, but quantify the light-element risk in Al matrices. | (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000029) |
| **Expertise dependence and sparse rationale** | Operators remain responsible for checking artefact peaks and background fits. EBSD quality definitions are vendor-specific and partly documented; parameter effects remain incompletely quantified. Manufacturer-constrained software prevented investigators from identifying why two systems produced different standardless accuracies. | Standards and vendor defaults provide thresholds and operating envelopes, but not transparent alloy-specific response models; optimization remains instrument-, specimen-, and operator-dependent. | (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) |
