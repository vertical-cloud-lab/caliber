(pqac-00000019) | Lattice parameter can indicate supersaturation, but it is not uniquely a Si assay: other solutes, vacancies, defects, temperature, calibration, and the chosen composition–lattice model contribute. |

(pqac-00000024) | XRD can distinguish crystalline Al and Si reflections and quantify crystalline phase fractions by a validated Rietveld model, subject to texture, overlap, crystallinity, and model quality. | AlSi10Mg contained **5–30 nm crystalline Si plus amorphous/disordered Si**, and 5–10 nm Si particles in α-Al. Amorphous Si gives no EBSD crystal pattern, while these dimensions are below or near practical conventional-EBSD resolution. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000060) |
| Grain size versus “crystallite size” | EBSD grain size is reconstructed spatially from neighboring pixels separated by a chosen misorientation criterion; it can be related directly to the sectioned particle diameter and used to count grains intersected by that section. | Scherrer or Williamson–Hall “crystallite size” is a volume-weighted coherent diffraction-domain size inferred from peak breadth—not necessarily the metallographic grain size and never the powder-particle diameter. | A grain may contain several dendrite arms or multiple coherent domains; a 2D section can also miss off-plane grains, so neither EBSD nor XRD alone gives an exact 3D grain count per particle. |
| Detection limits and sampling | Detectability depends on step size, interaction volume, pattern quality, surface preparation, phase dimensions, indexing library, and mapped area. Small or strained phases may be unindexed or misindexed; rare phases can nevertheless be found if deliberately targeted. | There is **no universal wt% detection limit**. A few wt% is a common laboratory-scale expectation, not a guarantee: isolated sharp peaks can reveal less, whereas overlap, fluorescence, texture, poor crystallinity, or amorphous material can raise the limit. Rietveld quantification likewise requires an adequate phase model. (pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) | Vanzetti et al. did not detect the predicted approximately 1% Q phase by XRD, while TEM/EDS suggested Q-like precipitates; this illustrates why a nominal threshold cannot replace system-specific validation. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000026) |
| Sampling statistics | Usually tens of sectioned particles, unless a large mount and automated mapping are used; statistics must report particles mapped, section-selection rules, indexed fraction, and size classes. | A conventional specimen uses a macroscopic powder aliquot and therefore samples vastly more particles and crystallites than a typical EBSD map, but only as an ensemble average; segregation during mounting and inadequate grain averaging remain possible. | Use EBSD for particle-resolved heterogeneity and XRD for population-average phase/lattice information; replicate mounts or aliquots are needed for either method when the lot is heterogeneous. |
