**Crystallographic texture** (ODF, pole figures, texture index) stands at the top of this ranking. It is the single most widely demanded EBSD output for both research and industry because it directly governs macroscopic elastic and plastic anisotropy through the fundamental relationship between crystal orientation and slip system activation described by Schmid's law (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 8-10). Texture also serves as the primary input for crystal plasticity modeling pipelines (wanni2024machinelearningenhanced pages 1-2). Measuring distances between crystallographic textures is recognized as essential for materials design and optimal process control in the metal industry (iraki2024accuratedistancesmeasures pages 1-3).

**Grain size and grain size distribution** is the second most important quantity, underpinning the Hall-Petch relationship and serving as a mandatory input for generating initial microstructures in full-field modeling and for material parameter identification associated with microstructure evolution (flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2, lakshmanan2022crystalplasticityconstitutive pages 138-143). Volume-weighted average grain size has been shown to produce a better-fitting Hall-Petch relationship compared to simple number-averaged grain size (flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2).

**Misorientation distribution and GBCD** rank third. The fraction, connectivity, and character of grain boundaries—particularly the distinction between special (CSL) and random high-angle boundaries—has direct, quantitative implications for intergranular corrosion, stress corrosion cracking, and corrosion fatigue (zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 4-6, barr2018trackingtheevolution pages 2-3, zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 1-2, liu2019grainboundaryengineering pages 6-8).

**KAM, GOS, and GND density** are among the most frequently reported EBSD metrics in the literature, used to characterize local plastic strain, deformation heterogeneity, and recrystallization state (roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 34-43, flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2). However, their property linkage is weaker than often assumed, and they suffer from significant step-size dependence, placing them lower in the overall importance ranking for decision-making purposes.

**HR-EBSD** for residual elastic strain/stress measurement offers strain sensitivity of at least 1 × 10⁻⁴ (ball2023pergrainandneighbourhood pages 3-4) and has demonstrated capability for mapping crack-tip stress fields and dislocation structures (roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 43-46, roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 11-15), but remains a specialist technique with high acquisition burden and limited standardization.

**3D EBSD** serial sectioning provides uniquely valuable true connectivity and five-parameter grain boundary plane data. The five-parameter grain boundary character distribution (GBCD) relates grain boundary area to energy and reveals that frequently observed boundary planes tend to have the lowest energy (marquardt2018thestructureand pages 9-13, dillon2022grainboundaryenergies pages 2-2, marquardt2018thestructureand pages 6-9, dillon2022grainboundaryenergies pages 3-3). However, 3D EBSD datasets remain rare and resource-intensive.

## 2. Structure–Property Linkage

### Strongest demonstrated property payoff

**Texture → anisotropy.** Crystallographic texture is the primary source of both elastic and plastic anisotropy. In LPBF 316L stainless steel, moderate texture produces ~15% yield-strength variation between build directions (X: 581 MPa vs Z: 506–514 MPa), while extreme Goss texture (21 m.u.d.) creates ~30% strength differences between ⟨111⟩ and ⟨100⟩ directions (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12). Crystal plasticity models calibrated with EBSD texture data can predict stress–strain response within 5% error. Elongation varies from 36.2% to 96.3% depending on crystal orientation relative to load, further demonstrating that texture dominates over grain morphology and porosity as the primary source of mechanical anisotropy in AM metals (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 12-15, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 25-27).

**Grain size → yield strength (Hall-Petch).** The fundamental grain-size–strength relationship is well established. EBSD enables not only mean grain size measurement but full grain size distribution characterization, with the volume-weighted average providing the most physically meaningful input for Hall-Petch predictions (flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2). In crystal plasticity frameworks, EBSD-measured grain sizes are used to calculate slip-system-level micro-Hall-Petch parameters via the Saltykov method for 2D-to-3D conversion (lakshmanan2022crystalplasticityconstitutive pages 138-143).

**GBCD → corrosion and SCC.** Grain boundary engineering to increase the fraction of special boundaries (particularly Σ3) to ~74% has demonstrated strong improvements in SCC resistance. In 304 stainless steel, GBE treatment produced fewer secondary cracks and more tortuous intergranular crack paths (liu2019grainboundaryengineering pages 6-8). In 316L, optimal GBE conditions achieved the longest corrosion fatigue life of 3,187 cycles, with Σ3 boundaries showing ~3.5× less corrosion depth than random high-angle boundaries (210 nm vs 60 nm) (zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 4-6, zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 1-2). However, special boundaries resist intergranular corrosion but do not effectively block transgranular fatigue crack propagation (zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 4-6). On copper, EBSD revealed that the passivation efficiency of Σ3 boundaries transitions sharply at deviation angles of 0.4–0.5° from ideal coherent geometry (bettayeb2019combinedinsitu pages 9-13, bettayeb2019combinedinsitu pages 13-18).

**Orientation and Schmid factor → fatigue crack initiation.** In rolled Ti-6Al-4V, EBSD analysis of 596 crack segments showed that fatigue cracks initiate preferentially on prismatic slip systems in grains with very high Schmid factors (briffod2019effectofcrystallographic pages 24-28, briffod2019effectofcrystallographic pages 7-11, briffod2019effectofcrystallographic pages 1-4). At grain boundaries, crack deflection is governed by the interplay between the geometric compatibility factor and the Schmid factor in neighboring grains (briffod2019effectofcrystallographic pages 24-28). This demonstrates a strong, quantitative link between EBSD-measured local orientation data and fatigue crack path prediction.

**Prior-austenite grain size → toughness and martensite hierarchy.** In LPBF 17-4 PH steel, smaller prior austenite grains (~10.2 µm) led to improved grain boundary connectivity and higher length fractions of desirable intervariant boundaries (60°/[11̄1] and 10.5°/[011]), with implications for toughness, strength, intergranular corrosion, and cracking behavior (haines2025onthemartensitic pages 1-3, haines2025onthemartensitic pages 13-15). Martensitic transformation increases mechanical strength through Hall-Petch hardening via hierarchical subdivision of parent grains into child domains (niessen2022parentgrainreconstruction pages 1-4).

### Most often reported but weakly linked

**KAM/GOS/GND density** are extremely widely reported as qualitative indicators of deformation state, but their quantitative structure–property predictions remain weak because the metrics are inherently step-size dependent and lack standardized reporting conventions, making cross-study comparison unreliable (roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 34-43, flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2).

**Grain morphology (aspect ratio)** is frequently reported in AM studies, but its isolated contribution to mechanical properties is typically secondary to texture effects. In multiple LPBF alloy reviews, texture was identified as a greater contributor to strength anisotropy than grain shape alone (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 12-15).

## 3. Emerging and High-Growth Uses (2020–2026)

### CPFEM and ICME

The primary EBSD inputs for crystal plasticity simulations are crystallographic orientation data (Euler angles), grain size distributions, and grain morphology. EBSD maps are used to construct representative volume elements (RVEs), with each voxel assigned a distinct orientation from the measured texture (lakshmanan2022crystalplasticityconstitutive pages 74-81). The PRISMS-Plasticity pipeline, for example, generates starting microstructures from input EBSD data or synthetic textures for Taylor-type crystal plasticity modeling (wanni2024machinelearningenhanced pages 1-2). Accurate reduced-order Euler angle datasets that faithfully represent crystallographic texture are essential for these workflows; the TACS (texture adaptive clustering and sampling) method achieved Kolmogorov-Smirnov test scores near 0.9 for texture fidelity, compared to below 0.3 for conventional EBSD dataset reduction (wanni2024machinelearningenhanced pages 1-2).

### Synthetic microstructure generation

DREAM.3D and related tools require EBSD-derived orientation distributions and grain size distributions (including 2D-to-3D conversion via the Saltykov method) as input for generating synthetic polycrystalline microstructures (lakshmanan2022crystalplasticityconstitutive pages 138-143). Grain morphology (shape parameters) is also needed but is more difficult to standardize. The availability of high-quality EBSD texture and grain-size data is the actual bottleneck for these workflows, rather than the computational tools themselves.

### Machine learning surrogate models

ML approaches increasingly use texture representations (ODFs, pole figure images, orientation histograms) as features for microstructure–property regression. Novel distance measures based on Earth Mover's Distance have been introduced for quantifying texture similarity, which is essential for materials design and process optimization applications (iraki2024accuratedistancesmeasures pages 1-3). Transfer learning approaches have been applied to EBSD pattern classification (iraki2024accuratedistancesmeasures pages 1-3).

### Digital twins and AM qualification

For additive manufacturing, the dominant EBSD-derived inputs to digital twin and qualification frameworks are crystallographic texture (which controls anisotropy), grain morphology (columnar vs equiaxed), and defect/porosity metrics. Physics-based models require EBSD texture measurements to isolate and predict the contribution of each anisotropy source to specific alloys and LPBF processes (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 25-27).

## 4. Additive Manufacturing Specifically

### Most reported and decision-relevant EBSD outcomes for LPBF

**Crystallographic texture** is identified as the most critical EBSD measurement for AM process qualification, as it is the primary source of elastic and plastic anisotropy in virtually all LPBF metals (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 8-10, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 12-15, weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 25-27). In LPBF 316L, texture measured by EBSD directly predicts yield-stress and elongation anisotropy and can be used to calibrate crystal plasticity simulations (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12). For LPBF Ti-6Al-4V, reconstructed β-phase texture from EBSD reveals ⟨100⟩ fiber texture aligned with the build direction, with alignment disappearing after heat treatment above the α→β transformation temperature (niessen2022parentgrainreconstruction pages 27-30).

**Columnar vs equiaxed grain morphology** is the second most-reported outcome. Grain elongation in the solidification direction shows periodic variation from melting/remelting patterns; preferentially oriented grains grow at the expense of others, creating wide or bimodal size distributions (mingard2023theuseof pages 15-20). The NPL guide emphasizes that single mean values of grain size can be meaningless for AM materials without proper context about the distribution and calculation method used (mingard2023theuseof pages 15-20, mingard2023theuseof pages 1-8).

**Cell/subgrain structure in AlSi10Mg.** The as-built SLM AlSi10Mg microstructure consists of heterogeneous zones: fine melt-pool cells, coarse melt-pool regions, and heat-affected zones, all composed of primary α-Al cells surrounded by a continuous cellular Si network (snopinski2022evolutionofmicrostructure pages 4-6). EBSD combined with XRD tracks cell size evolution through heat treatment and severe plastic deformation, with ECAP reducing grain size from ~1.42 µm to ~0.24 µm after two passes (snopinski2022evolutionofmicrostructure pages 4-6). The corrosion resistance of AlSi10Mg improved significantly after ECAP processing, with polarization resistance increasing from ~1.1 kΩ·cm² (as-built) to ~75.5 kΩ·cm² (2 ECAP passes) (snopinski2022evolutionofmicrostructure pages 9-12).

**Melt-pool boundary structure and LAGB/HAGB fractions** are widely reported but their isolated contribution to properties is difficult to separate from texture effects. Melt pools primarily affect elongation and ductility rather than strength (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 25-27).

**Prior-austenite reconstruction in LPBF steels** is emerging as a high-value technique. In LPBF 17-4 PH steel, five-parameter characterization of intervariant boundaries showed that smaller prior austenite grains improve the grain boundary network by increasing desirable {110} twist boundary plane populations, which are beneficial for controlling hydrogen embrittlement and dislocation plasticity (haines2025onthemartensitic pages 1-3, haines2025onthemartensitic pages 13-15).

### Key findings on robustness vs fragility

**Most robust:** Area-weighted grain size metrics are the most reproducible EBSD outputs; they reduce dependence on step size and map quality, and better discriminate between samples with different size distributions than number-average metrics (mingard2023theuseof pages 24-29). Crystallographic texture is moderately robust if sample axis registration is rigorously controlled (mingard2023theuseof pages 8-15).

**Most fragile:** KAM, GOS, and GND density are explicitly step-size dependent. Local misorientation values scale with step size, and GND density calculations amplify orientation noise because they require spatial gradients (flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2, roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 11-15). HR-EBSD strain mapping depends critically on reference pattern selection and surface preparation quality (ball2023pergrainandneighbourhood pages 3-4). Grain morphology parameters (aspect ratio, ellipticity, circularity) are highly definition-dependent: two commercial software packages analyzing the same AM sample produced ~20% differences in fitted major-axis values and reported inverse aspect-ratio conventions (3.3 vs 0.27), potentially inverting interpretation if not carefully documented (mingard2023theuseof pages 29-33). 3D EBSD statistics are fragile due to registration, segmentation tolerance, and slice-loss sensitivity (mingard2023theuseof pages 1-8, dillon2022grainboundaryenergies pages 1-2).

**Standards landscape:** ISO 13067:2020 covers average grain size from EBSD, and ASTM E2627 is commonly referenced for EBSD grain-size practice (mingard2023theuseof pages 1-8, mingard2023theuseof pages 33-41). No mature universal standards exist for KAM/GOS/GND reporting, HR-EBSD stress mapping, five-parameter GBCD, or grain morphology parameter definitions. The NPL Good Practice Guide (Mingard 2023) specifically warns that different EBSD software packages use different definitions for shape parameters and color scales, and that these choices can materially alter conclusions about sample-to-sample differences (mingard2023theuseof pages 1-8, mingard2023theuseof pages 29-33, mingard2023theuseof pages 33-41).

### Summary rationale

**Tier 1 (Highest Value)** comprises crystallographic texture, grain size/distribution, and grain morphology/aspect ratio. These three outputs together form the minimal necessary input set for CPFEM, synthetic microstructure generation, and AM process qualification. They have the strongest demonstrated property payoff (anisotropy, Hall-Petch, columnar-vs-equiaxed classification), the highest computational demand, and—critically—their reproducibility weaknesses are addressable through standardized definitions, consistent thresholds, and area-weighted metrics (weaver2021understandinganisotropictensile pages 10-12, flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2, mingard2023theuseof pages 1-8, mingard2023theuseof pages 24-29, mingard2023theuseof pages 29-33, wanni2024machinelearningenhanced pages 1-2).

**Tier 2 (High Value)** includes GBCD/misorientation distribution, phase fraction/identification, and parent-phase reconstruction. These outputs have strong property linkage in specific but industrially critical domains (stainless steels for corrosion, multiphase alloys, martensitic steels and Ti alloys), and rising demand in AM qualification. Their lower ranking reflects greater sensitivity to tolerance choices and workflow dependence (zhang2022effectsofgrain pages 4-6, liu2019grainboundaryengineering pages 6-8, niessen2022parentgrainreconstruction pages 27-30, haines2025onthemartensitic pages 1-3).

**Tier 3 (Moderate Value)** covers KAM/GOS/GND density and twin/variant analysis. Although extremely popular in publications, KAM and GND density are poor early-stage automation targets because their inherent step-size dependence means that optimized acquisition parameters will change the absolute values of these metrics, creating a reproducibility paradox unless uncertainty is propagated explicitly (roy2026themechanisticaspects pages 34-43, flipon2021grainsizecharacterization pages 1-2).

**Tier 4 (Premature/Low Value for general-purpose automation)** includes HR-EBSD strain mapping and 3D EBSD statistics. Both are scientifically powerful but too fragile, labor-intensive, and under-standardized for broad automated optimization at present (ball2023pergrainandneighbourhood pages 3-4, dillon2022grainboundaryenergies pages 1-2). They are best pursued as later-stage extensions after robust 2D workflows are established.

In conclusion, an automated EBSD parameter-optimization tool would create the most immediate scientific and industrial value by targeting crystallographic texture, grain size distribution (with area-weighted metrics and explicit threshold reporting per ISO 13067), and standardized grain morphology descriptors as its primary outputs. Misorientation/GBCD and phase identification are strong secondary targets. KAM/GOS/GND, while frequently demanded, should only be automated once their step-size dependence is explicitly addressed through uncertainty quantification. HR-EBSD and 3D EBSD are best reserved for specialized deployment.
